# Computational biology pathfinder scope

## Purpose and audience

The Computational Biology Pathfinder is for motivated high-school students who know some introductory biology but may not know how computational biologists turn broad curiosity into researchable directions. In an approximately 8–12 minute, peer-guided experience, it should help a student identify one promising direction, two nearby alternatives, and practical language for beginning a literature search.

The pathfinder is an orientation tool. It does not grade biology knowledge, diagnose ability, choose a final research question, recommend medical treatment, or claim that one field is a perfect match.

## Scientific coverage

The final taxonomy should provide balanced entry points across:

- comparative, population, and pathogen genomics;
- molecular evolution and phylogenetics;
- gene expression, regulation, single-cell analysis, and spatial biology;
- protein sequence, structure, function, interactions, and biomolecular modeling;
- computational therapeutic discovery and biomedical genomics;
- systems biology, biological networks, and metabolic modeling;
- microbiomes, metagenomics, biodiversity, and computational ecology; and
- machine learning, data integration, and evaluation of bioinformatics methods.

Directions should be narrow enough to support a useful literature search but broad enough for a beginner to compare questions, systems, datasets, and methods before selecting a project.

## Educational boundaries

- Familiarity, mathematics, statistics, coding, and tool experience affect preparation guidance only. They must never make a direction less worthy or inaccessible.
- Uncertainty keeps options open and must not create a negative score.
- Biomedical examples remain educational and research-focused. Results must not interpret personal health data or provide clinical advice.
- Example systems and search language should be scientifically grounded without inventing paper citations.
- Search guidance must remind students to verify bibliographic details and read the real source.
- Human, animal, environmental, and pathogen data should be discussed with appropriate attention to privacy, bias, consent, and responsible interpretation.

## Student experience

The finished adaptive survey should usually present 14–18 questions. It begins with a non-graded check-in, follows the student’s broad motivation with relevant narrowing questions, and finishes with preferences about biological scale, evidence, modeling, and workflow.

Results should clearly separate:

1. interest fit;
2. research-style fit; and
3. preparation considerations.

The result is a reading direction, not a permanent label. Students should be able to review answers, resolve contradictions, explore a nearby direction, export a consistent research profile, restart safely, and return after refreshing the browser.

## Release status

The complete module is available at `/pathfinders/computational-biology`. It includes 24 directions, conditional narrowing for every motivation family, deterministic scoring, preparation guidance, three literature-search launchpads, plain-text export, isolated local persistence, answer review, and nearby-direction selection. See `computational-biology-maintenance.md` for editing and release guidance.
