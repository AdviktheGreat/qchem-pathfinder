# Computational physics pathfinder scope

## Purpose and audience

The Computational Physics Pathfinder is for motivated high-school students who know some introductory physics but may not know how computational physicists turn broad curiosity into research directions. In an approximately 8–12 minute, peer-guided experience, it should help a student identify one promising direction, two nearby alternatives, and practical language for beginning a literature search.

The pathfinder is an orientation tool. It does not grade physics knowledge, measure mathematical talent, select a final research question, or claim that one field is a perfect match.

## Scientific coverage

The final taxonomy should provide balanced entry points across:

- astrophysical, orbital, gravitational, and cosmological simulation;
- fluid dynamics, turbulence, and geophysical flows;
- plasma, fusion, magnetohydrodynamic, and space-physics modeling;
- condensed-matter, many-body, magnetic, and topological physics;
- atomic, optical, quantum-dynamics, and open-quantum-system simulation;
- statistical mechanics, nonlinear dynamics, and complex systems;
- particle, detector, nuclear, and lattice-based computation; and
- numerical methods, inverse problems, uncertainty, high-performance computing, and scientific machine learning.

Directions should be narrow enough to support a useful literature search but broad enough for a beginner to compare systems, physical questions, mathematical models, and computational methods before selecting a project.

## Boundaries with neighboring pathfinders

- Quantum Chemistry remains the primary home for molecular electronic structure, chemical bonding, spectra, and reaction pathways.
- Computational Materials remains the primary home for designing or predicting the behavior of useful material families.
- Computational Biology remains the primary home for biological data, biomolecular function, genomics, cells, and ecosystems.
- Computational Physics may include neighboring phenomena when the central question concerns physical laws, dynamics, fields, particles, or numerical modeling rather than the neighboring field’s primary research goal.

## Educational principles

- Familiarity, mathematics, coding, and tool experience affect preparation guidance only. They must never make a direction less worthy or inaccessible.
- Uncertainty keeps possibilities open and must not create a negative score.
- Models should be presented as purposeful approximations with assumptions, resolution limits, and validation needs—not as exact copies of nature.
- Numerical output is evidence to interpret, not an automatic physical explanation.
- Examples should be scientifically grounded without inventing paper citations.
- Search guidance must remind students to verify bibliographic details and read the real source.

## Student experience

The finished adaptive survey should usually present 14–18 questions. It begins with a non-graded check-in, follows the student’s broad motivation with relevant narrowing questions, and finishes with preferences about physical scale, evidence, mathematical description, simulation, and workflow.

Results should clearly separate:

1. interest fit;
2. research-style fit; and
3. preparation considerations.

The result is a reading direction, not a permanent label. Students should be able to review answers, resolve contradictions, explore a nearby direction, export a consistent research profile, restart safely, and return after refreshing the browser.

## Phase 1 boundary

Phase 1 defines the identity, introduction, calibration, broad motivations, common research preferences, glossary, persistence namespace, and validation rules. It does not add the final taxonomy, recommendation affinities, adaptive narrowing branches, public route, or hub launch. The catalog remains marked as coming soon until the complete experience is ready.
